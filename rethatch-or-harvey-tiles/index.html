Re-thatch or Convert to Harvey Thatch Tiles?

How to tell if your lapa needs a repair, a brush, a full re-thatch, or a switch to Harvey thatch tiles.

Send George a photo

Signs your thatch needs work

  • Water coming through in the rain
  • Grey, flat or patchy grass
  • Thin spots where you can see the battens
  • Grass falling off or birds nesting in it
  • Moss or rot around the bottom edge

Repair, brush or re-thatch

If the thatch is only grey and tired, brushing it brings it back. If it leaks in one place, the spot can be repaired. If the grass is thin and rotten all over, it needs a full re-thatch.

Or convert to Harvey thatch tiles

If you're tired of brushing and re-thatching, you can switch to Harvey thatch tiles. They look like thatch, so the lapa keeps its look, but they need far less maintenance. We fit them over the existing lapa structure.

Questions

Is it cheaper to re-thatch or convert to Harvey thatch tiles?

Re-thatching usually costs less upfront. Harvey thatch tiles cost more on the day but need far less upkeep afterwards. [George to confirm typical prices for both.]

Can you put Harvey tiles on my existing lapa?

Usually yes. We fit new battens over the existing poles and roof structure. If poles or beams are rotten, we replace them first.

Will it still look like a lapa?

Yes. Harvey thatch tiles are made to look like thatch, so the lapa keeps its shape and its look.

Does it affect my insurance?

Thatch roofs are often treated as a fire risk by insurers. Ask your insurer how a tiled lapa changes your premium. [Check with your insurer.]
